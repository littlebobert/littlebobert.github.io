Privacy Policy

Kaketa

Overview

Last updated: October 10, 2026

Kaketa has no account and no analytics. Your practice progress and the words you add stay on your device and in your own iCloud. The handwriting check is the one feature that sends anything off your device: when you tap Check, a picture of your handwriting goes through a small server to OpenAI, as described below.

Data the app handles

  • Which words you have marked known or learning, and the review schedule and counts for each.
  • The words you add under My Words.
  • Your settings, such as the prompt and the meaning language.

What you draw on the practice grid is kept only for the current card and is discarded when you move on.

Storage and sync

Progress, My Words, and settings are stored on your device. Progress and My Words also sync through iCloud, in your private iCloud database, between devices signed in to the same Apple Account. Apple handles that storage under its own terms; we cannot read it. The API key is stored in the device Keychain and is not synced.

Handwriting check

When the handwriting check is on and you tap Check, Kaketa sends a PNG image of what you drew on the grid and the word you were asked to write (its spelling, reading, and meaning) to Kaketa’s check server, a small service run by Kaketa’s developer on Cloudflare. The server passes them to OpenAI’s Decisions API with the developer’s key and returns one of three answers: correct, close, or wrong. Nothing is sent unless you tap Check.

The server stores nothing about a check: not the image, not the word, and not the answer. It keeps only a count of checks per device for the daily allowance (300 a day) and a monthly total, plus the public key Apple’s App Attest gives your copy of the app, which proves requests come from the real app. OpenAI processes the request under its own terms and privacy policy.

No data collection, sharing, or tracking by the app

Kaketa does not transmit app data to us, collect it on a server, share it with third parties, or track you across apps and websites. The app sends no analytics to us. Apple gives us aggregated App Store statistics, and, if you have turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings, aggregated usage figures and crash reports; these come from Apple and do not identify you. The kaketa.jp website does not track which links you click.
