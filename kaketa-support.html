Support

Kaketa

Quick start

  1. Open Kaketa and tap Start on the Practice tab. It gives you ten new words from Grade 1; change the group under “New words from”.
  2. Read the prompt, write the word in the squares with Apple Pencil or a finger (or write it on paper), then tap Show Answer.
  3. Compare with the answer and its stroke order, then tap Got It or Not Yet. If you already knew the word, tap “I already know this word” and it won’t come up again.

Skipping words you already know

  • In Words, open a group and a section, tap Select, pick the words, and tap Mark Mastered. Select All marks the whole section.
  • To mark a whole group, such as all of Grade 1, open the group and use the ⋯ menu: Mark All as Mastered.
  • Swipe a word to the right to mark it mastered, or back to learning. A word’s page has a Status button too.

Handwriting check

  • In Settings → Handwriting Check, turn on Check handwriting. A Check button then appears while practicing, and OpenAI says whether what you wrote matches: correct, close, or not quite.
  • It’s free, up to 300 checks a day per device, through Kaketa’s server, which keeps nothing. OpenAI may keep what it receives for up to 30 days to monitor for abuse. Tap Test the Connection to make sure it works. It needs a real iPhone or iPad; it isn’t available in the simulator.
  • If the check says it isn’t available, OpenAI’s Decisions API (which is in beta) is unreachable for the moment; grade yourself and try later.

Sync

  • Progress and My Words sync through iCloud between devices signed in to the same Apple Account. There is nothing to sign up for; make sure iCloud Drive is on in iOS Settings.
  • Sync can take a minute after you make a change. The API key for the handwriting check does not sync; enter it on each device.

Requirements and limitations

  • Requires iOS or iPadOS 27 or later.
  • The grade groups follow the kanji taught in each year of Japanese elementary school; the words for each are common words, not an official school list. JLPT levels are community lists, since the test publishes none.
  • Meanings in Japanese and Tagalog are machine translated; English is from the dictionaries directly.
