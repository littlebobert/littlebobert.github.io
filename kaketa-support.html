Kaketa

Quick start

  1. Open Kaketa and tap Start on the Practice tab. It gives you ten new words from Grade 1.
  2. Read the prompt, write the word in the squares with Apple Pencil or a finger, then tap Show Answer.
  3. Compare with the answer and its stroke order, then tap Got It or Not Yet. If you already knew the word, tap “I have already mastered this word” and it won’t come up again.

Skipping words you already know

  • In Words, open a group and a section, tap Select, pick the words, and tap Mark Mastered. Select All marks the whole section.
  • To mark a whole group, such as all of Grade 1, open the group and use the ⋯ menu: Mark All as Mastered.
  • Swipe a word to the right to mark it mastered, or back to learning. A word’s page has a Status button too.

Handwriting check

  • The check is built in and runs on your device: when you show the answer, Kaketa compares each stroke you drew with the standard strokes and says whether the word looks right, is close, or isn’t quite there. Strokes that are missing or drawn backwards are marked on the grid.
  • If the check feels too hard or too easy, change its strictness. Standard forgives one slip per character and treats a backwards stroke as a lesser one; Loose forgives two and ignores direction; Strict counts a backwards stroke against you. It’s in Settings → Handwriting Check, and on the answer itself, where changing it judges the word again at once.

Sync

  • Progress and My Words sync through iCloud between devices signed in to the same Apple Account. There is nothing to sign up for; make sure iCloud Drive is on in iOS Settings.
  • Sync can take a minute after you make a change.

Requirements and limitations

  • Requires iOS or iPadOS 26 or later.
  • The grade groups follow the kanji taught in each year of Japanese elementary school; the words for each are common words, not an official school list. JLPT levels are community lists, since the test publishes none.
  • Meanings in Japanese and Tagalog are machine translated; English is from the dictionaries directly.
