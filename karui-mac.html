Karui for Mac Beta

Makes Karui for iPhone even smarter
Download for macOS (version 0.8.6)
Free and open source under the MIT License, provided as is, without warranty. Needs macOS 15 on an Apple Silicon Mac, Karui 0.2.0 or later on your iPhone, and the same iCloud account on both.

Karui on a MacBook Air

Karui for Mac improves spam filtering on your iPhone.

Notifications that you read on your Mac clear on your iPhone.

Setting up

  • Open Karui for Mac. In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, press + and choose Karui.
  • That’s it. Your iPhone lists the Mac under Settings → Sorting & Alerts → Karui for Mac.

Privacy

Everything goes through the private part of your iCloud account, which only your devices can read. The Mac sends a fingerprint of each notification, its link, and whether you’ve read it, never its text. With History on your Mac turned on, your iPhone sends Karui’s decisions and each notification’s text, end-to-end encrypted. Addresses you’ve written to leave the Mac only as hashes. Links are deleted after a day; History stays on the Mac for a month.

Reading Notification Center’s database needs Full Disk Access. Only Messages, Spark, and Slack notifications are decoded. That’s a lot to ask of an app, which is why this one is open source.

View the source code on GitHub.

How it works

Karui for Mac watches Notification Center for Messages, Spark, and Slack, finds each notification’s message or email in that app’s own database, and sends your iPhone a link to it through iCloud. Tap Karui’s alert on the iPhone and the right thread opens. Read one on the Mac and the alert clears on the iPhone.

Once a day it sends hashes of the addresses you’ve written to in Spark and Messages, never the addresses themselves, so with “Alert for senders you’ve written to” turned on, their notifications get through like a saved contact’s. With History on your Mac turned on, the Mac shows your History too, and read marks, categories, and deletions carry over both ways.

The Mac has to be on and logged in; it can be locked.
