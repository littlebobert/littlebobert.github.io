Privacy Policy

Karui

Overview

Last updated: September 30, 2026

By default, Karui classifies notifications entirely on your iPhone. The app does not use an account, has no server or cloud service, and sends no analytics to us. The Second Opinion feature, off by default, sends limited data to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute as described below.

Data the app handles

  • Notification fields passed to Karui by the Shortcuts automations you create.
  • For selected VIP contacts, an on-device snapshot of name, email addresses, and phone numbers. Manual VIPs store the name you enter.
  • Upcoming calendar event title and time context, read on-device.
  • Your policy settings and local notification history.

On-device processing and storage

Classification uses Apple’s on-device Foundation Models when available, with a deterministic local fallback. Notification, VIP, and calendar context is processed on-device. VIP data, settings, and history are stored locally. If you turn on Second Opinion, your Karui Shortcut sends a notification’s text and app name, whether the app is one of your important apps, your names, and your sorting terms to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute, which returns a classification. By default this happens only when the on-device model could not sort the notification. With When to Ask set to Always, it happens for every notification the on-device model sorts, and when you rerun a classification in Karui. Notifications Karui decides by its own rules (from your important contacts, mentioning you, containing a one-time code, or matching your urgent terms), VIP contact details, and calendar events are never sent. Apple describes Private Cloud Compute’s privacy protections here.

No data collection, sharing, or tracking by the app

Karui does not transmit app data to us, collect it on a server, share it with third parties, or track you across apps and websites. The app sends no analytics to us. Apple and source apps may separately process data under their own terms when their features or services are used, including Private Cloud Compute when Second Opinion is on.

Connections to Apple

Karui’s own code makes no network requests. Apple’s App Store framework, which Karui uses to check whether it’s a TestFlight build, occasionally contacts Apple’s App Store (a domain under itunes.apple.com) and Apple’s certificate validation service (such as ocsp2.apple.com). iOS lists these under Karui in App Privacy Report. They verify Karui’s App Store download and don’t carry your notifications, contacts, calendar events, or settings. Apple chooses these domain names and may change them.

If you turn on translation, iOS may download Apple’s language packs; translation itself happens on your iPhone. Second Opinion requests are sent by iOS on behalf of your shortcut, as described above. Technical Note KN004 shows how to check all of this yourself.

Statistics from Apple

Apple gives us aggregated App Store statistics, such as how many people view Karui’s App Store page and how many download it. If you’ve turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings (you can turn it off in Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple also shares aggregated usage figures, such as how often Karui is opened and how many devices use it, and crash reports. These come from Apple, not from Karui, and don’t identify you. The karui.jp website doesn’t track which links you click.

Permissions

  • Contacts: used when you choose VIPs.
  • Calendar: used to read upcoming event title and time context on-device for classification.
  • Notifications: used to rebroadcast important notifications.

You can change permissions in iOS Settings.

Retention and deletion

Data remains on your device until you remove it through available app controls, reset settings or history, or delete the app. You can remove VIPs in the app. Deleting Karui removes its local app data through the standard iOS process, subject to how iOS manages backups and system data.

Children and general use

Karui is a general-purpose utility and is not directed to children. Because the app does not collect or transmit personal data to us, we do not knowingly collect children’s personal data through the app.
