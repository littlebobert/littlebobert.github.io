Privacy Policy

Karui

Overview

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Karui classifies notifications on your iPhone. The app does not use an account, has no server or cloud service of ours, and sends no analytics to us. The Second Opinion feature, which setup offers turned on and you can turn off at any time, sends limited data to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute as described below. If you use Karui for Mac, the two apps exchange data through your own iCloud account, as described below.

Data the app handles

  • Notification fields passed to Karui by the Shortcuts automations you create.
  • For selected VIP contacts, an on-device snapshot of name, email addresses, and phone numbers. Manual VIPs store the name you enter.
  • Upcoming calendar event title and time context, read on-device.
  • Your policy settings and local notification history.
  • With Karui for Mac, links, read marks, and sender hashes from the Mac, notifications only the Mac received if you send them to your iPhone, and, if you turn it on, your History and sorting settings sent to the Mac, all through your iCloud account.

On-device processing and storage

Classification uses Apple’s on-device Foundation Models when available, with a deterministic local fallback. Notification, VIP, and calendar context is processed on-device. VIP data, settings, and history are stored locally. If you turn on Second Opinion, Karui sends a notification’s text and app name, whether the app is one of your important apps, your names, and your sorting terms to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute, which returns a classification. Karui asks directly when it can, and through your Karui Shortcut while Apple Intelligence on your iPhone is busy; Karui for Mac asks through a Shortcut of your own on the Mac. By default this happens only when the on-device model could not sort the notification. With When to Ask set to Before Alerting, it happens for every notification the on-device model sorts, and when you rerun a classification in Karui. Notifications Karui decides by its own rules (from your important contacts, mentioning you, containing a one-time code, or matching your urgent terms), VIP contact details, and calendar events are never sent. Apple describes Private Cloud Compute’s privacy protections here.

No data collection, sharing, or tracking by the app

Karui does not transmit app data to us, collect it on a server, share it with third parties, or track you across apps and websites. The app sends no analytics to us. Apple and source apps may separately process data under their own terms when their features or services are used, including Private Cloud Compute when Second Opinion is on, and iCloud when you use Karui for Mac.

Connections to Apple

Karui’s own code makes no network requests of its own; the connections here are Apple’s frameworks working on Karui’s behalf. With Second Opinion on, Apple’s Foundation Models framework sends Karui’s requests to Private Cloud Compute, as described above. With Karui for Mac, it reads and writes your iCloud account through Apple’s CloudKit, as described below. Apple’s App Store framework, which Karui uses to check whether it’s a TestFlight build, occasionally contacts Apple’s App Store (a domain under itunes.apple.com) and Apple’s certificate validation service (such as ocsp2.apple.com). iOS lists these under Karui in App Privacy Report. They verify Karui’s App Store download and don’t carry your notifications, contacts, calendar events, or settings. Apple chooses these domain names and may change them.

If you turn on translation, iOS may download Apple’s language packs; translation itself happens on your iPhone. Second Opinion requests Karui can’t make itself are sent by iOS on behalf of your shortcut, as described above. Technical Note KN004 shows how to check all of this yourself.

Karui for Mac and iCloud

Karui for Mac is a separate, optional app. When it runs on a Mac signed in to the same iCloud account, the two apps exchange data through the private database of your iCloud account, which only your devices can read. There is no server of ours in between, and nothing is shared with anyone else. If you don’t use Karui for Mac, the iPhone app writes nothing to iCloud.

From the Mac: a fingerprint of each notification (a hash of its app, title, and text, not the text itself), a link to the message or email it came from, and whether you’ve read it, changed its category, or deleted it on the Mac. The Mac’s name and app version, so the iPhone knows a Mac is active. While you’re using the Mac, only that you are and when, so Karui on your iPhone sends its alerts quietly meanwhile; “Quiet your iPhone while you use this Mac” in Karui for Mac’s settings turns this off. If you turn on “Alert for senders you’ve written to”, hashes of the addresses, numbers, and names of people you’ve written to in Spark and Messages on the Mac, never the addresses themselves. If you turn on “Send notifications only this Mac received to your iPhone”, the title, subtitle, and text of a notification only the Mac received, when it arrived, and the Mac’s own decision about it, with the text and the decision’s reason stored with end-to-end encryption, so Karui on your iPhone can sort it and alert you. Links are deleted after a day.

From the iPhone, only with “Show History on your Mac” turned on: Karui’s decision about each notification (its category, the model’s interest, when it was sorted, and whether it’s unread) with the app’s name, and the notification’s title, text, subtitle, sender, and recipients, so the Mac can show your History. The text, reason, subtitle, sender, and recipients are stored with end-to-end encryption, which Apple cannot read. Records are updated for a day and deleted when you delete the item from History; the Mac keeps its copy for a month. With the same setting on, the iPhone also sends its sorting settings, including your VIPs’ names, numbers, and email addresses but not their photos, your names and terms, and what Karui learned from your fixes as hashes of short pieces of text, never the text, all with end-to-end encryption, so Karui for Mac sorts notifications as your iPhone does. Turning the setting off deletes them from iCloud, and Karui for Mac then deletes its copy.

Apple processes iCloud data under its own terms. Karui for Mac’s page describes what it reads on the Mac.

Statistics from Apple

Apple gives us aggregated App Store statistics, such as how many people view Karui’s App Store page and how many download it. If you’ve turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings (you can turn it off in Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple also shares aggregated usage figures, such as how often Karui is opened and how many devices use it, and crash reports. These come from Apple, not from Karui, and don’t identify you. The karui.jp website doesn’t track which links you click.

Permissions

  • Contacts: used when you choose VIPs.
  • Calendar: used to read upcoming event title and time context on-device for classification.
  • Notifications: used to rebroadcast important notifications.

You can change permissions in iOS Settings.

Retention and deletion

Data remains on your device until you remove it through available app controls, reset settings or history, or delete the app. You can remove VIPs in the app. Deleting Karui removes its local app data through the standard iOS process, subject to how iOS manages backups and system data.

Children and general use

Karui is a general-purpose utility and is not directed to children. Because the app does not collect or transmit personal data to us, we do not knowingly collect children’s personal data through the app.
