Tech Specs

Karui

How it works

  1. A Focus silences the apps you choose and allows only Karui.
  2. For each notification from those apps, iOS 27’s Shortcuts trigger “When I receive a notification” runs the Karui Shortcut, which passes the notification’s app, title, text, and sender to Karui.
  3. Karui classifies the notification as Important, Neutral, or Spam.
  4. Karui posts Important notifications again, and your Focus lets them through. Tapping one opens the app it came from, or the conversation itself in Messages and WhatsApp when Karui has the sender’s number. With Karui for Mac on your Mac, it opens the exact email or message in Spark, Slack, or Messages. Neutral and Spam notifications stay quiet. Every notification is kept in History, and Neutral ones can also appear in a daily summary.

Classification

  • Rules on your device decide first. Messages from your important contacts, messages that mention one of your names, one-time codes, and messages matching your urgent terms are always Important.
  • Everything else goes to Apple’s on-device Foundation Models, with your names, terms, important apps, and upcoming calendar events as private context. The model returns a category, a confidence, a short reason, and an interest score from 1 to 5 that ranks the daily summary.
  • When the on-device model isn’t available, for example when iOS limits it while Karui runs in the background, transparent rules decide instead, and Karui rechecks those notifications later. See Technical Note KN001.
  • Second Opinion, offered at setup, asks Apple’s Private Cloud Compute when the on-device model couldn’t sort a notification, or after every on-device answer with When to Ask set to Before Alerting. Karui asks directly when it can, and through your Shortcut’s Use Model action while Apple Intelligence on your iPhone is busy. See Technical Note KN002.

Accuracy and speed

  • On-device classification usually takes about 4 seconds on iPhone 17 Pro. A Second Opinion usually adds 2 to 3 seconds, whether Karui asks or your Shortcut does.
  • iOS gives apps in the background a small share of the on-device model. After a burst of notifications, Karui may sort some with hard-coded rules instead of the model for a while, then rechecks them when you open Karui, or right away with Second Opinion on. Why this happens.
  • Karui can misclassify a notification. Check Notification Center and Karui’s History when something may have been missed.

Privacy

  • Karui has no server and no account, and sends no analytics. Your VIPs, settings, and History stay on your device unless you use Karui for Mac.
  • Second Opinion sends a notification’s text and app, your names, and your sorting terms to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute. Anything the rules decide, contact details, and calendar events are never sent. See the privacy policy.
  • With Karui for Mac, your iPhone and Mac exchange data through your own iCloud account, with no server in between: links and read marks from the Mac and, if you turn on Show History on your Mac, your History and sorting settings from the iPhone. Notification text and settings are end-to-end encrypted. Without the Mac app, Karui only checks iCloud for a Mac each time it opens. See Technical Note KN004.

Requirements

  • iOS or iPadOS 27 or later. Spam filtering and deadline reminders also need Apple Intelligence on: iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone Air, any iPhone 16 model, or later; iPad mini (A17 Pro), or an iPad Air or iPad Pro with M1 or later. Viewing unsent messages works without it. iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, and iPhone Air run Apple’s more capable on-device model.
  • The Shortcuts app with the standard Karui Shortcut, and a Focus that allows Karui.
