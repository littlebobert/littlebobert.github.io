KN004: Verifying What Karui Sends Off Your iPhone

Karui

How to check for yourself that Karui sends none of your data off your iPhone, which Apple domains it contacts and why, and what changes when you turn on Second Opinion.

September 28, 2026 · iOS 27.0

Overview

Karui is an on-device iPhone notification filter. It has no account and no server, and it sends no analytics. The app isn’t open source, and even if it were, you couldn’t easily confirm that the build from the App Store matches the published code. This note shows how to test the app installed on your iPhone.

What’s in the app

  • No networking code. Karui’s own code never makes a network request. The only connections come from Apple’s App Store framework, described below.
  • No third-party SDKs: no analytics, advertising, or crash reporting. Karui uses only Apple’s frameworks.
  • No iCloud sync. History, settings, and VIPs stay in the app’s own storage on your iPhone.
  • Diagnostic logs record timings, counts, and categories, never notification text. They stay in your iPhone’s system log and leave the device only if you share a diagnostics report yourself.
  • Crash reports reach me only if you’ve turned on Share iPhone Analytics and Share with App Developers in iOS Settings. Apple collects and anonymizes them, not Karui.

Check with App Privacy Report

iOS can record which domains each app contacts. Karui can’t hide from it or turn it off.

  1. Open Settings › Privacy & Security › App Privacy Report, and turn it on.
  2. Use your iPhone normally for a day or more, so Karui sorts notifications in the background too.
  3. Come back and look for Karui under App Network Activity.

You may see Karui there with the Apple domains described next, and nothing else. Language downloads for translation are made by iOS, not by Karui. Second Opinion requests reach Private Cloud Compute through iOS itself: in my report, with Second Opinion answering dozens of times a day, no app listed a Private Cloud Compute domain, not even Shortcuts.

Apple domains you may see

After several days with a TestFlight build, my App Privacy Report listed two domains under Karui, both Apple’s. Neither carries your notifications, contacts, calendar events, or settings. The connection counts depend on how often you open Karui’s Settings, since that’s when it asks StoreKit about the build; in a week of testing they were in the twenties.

  • mzstorekit.itunes.apple.com, or mzstorekit-sb.itunes.apple.com in TestFlight and Xcode builds: the App Store’s StoreKit service. Karui asks StoreKit whether it’s a TestFlight build, to show developer tools only there. StoreKit gets the answer from Apple as a signed record of your download and keeps a copy on your iPhone, so it only reaches the App Store now and then.
  • ocsp2.apple.com: certificate validation. iOS checks that a certificate hasn’t been revoked, most likely the one Apple signed that record with.

Both connections are made by Apple’s frameworks inside Karui, so iOS lists them under Karui. Many other App Store apps in the same report contacted mzstorekit.itunes.apple.com too.

The exact host names are Apple’s and may change. Apple lists the domains behind its services, including *.itunes.apple.com and ocsp2.apple.com, in Use Apple products on enterprise networks, and notes there that some of the names behind them are subject to change.

Calendar and contacts access

App Privacy Report also lists which data each app reads, under Data & Sensor Access. Karui may appear there. That’s reading on your iPhone, not sending anything:

  • Calendars: if you allow calendar access, Karui reads your upcoming events each time it sorts a notification, and for Auto Reply during meetings.
  • Contacts: Karui never reads your whole address book. When you add VIPs, in setup or in Settings, iOS’s contact picker gives Karui only the contacts you choose.

What Second Opinion sends

Second Opinion is off by default. When it’s on, your Karui shortcut makes the request, not Karui, with the Use Model action set to Private Cloud Compute. Open the shortcut in the Shortcuts app to see the step for yourself. KN002 describes the round trip.

The request contains the notification’s text and app name, whether the app is one of your important apps, your name, handles, and any nickname, and your sorting terms. Notifications Karui decides by its own rules, your VIP contact details, and your calendar events are never sent. If you delete that step or turn Second Opinion off, nothing leaves your iPhone.

Apple describes the protections in Private Cloud Compute on its security blog.

Watching traffic with a proxy

You can also route your iPhone through a proxy such as Charles or Proxyman. But keep these in mind: 1) a proxy shows only what happened while you watched, so App Privacy Report’s longer record is the better test, and 2) translation pack downloads come from iOS itself rather than from Karui.
