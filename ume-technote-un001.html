UN001: Testing Form Matching on 220 Forms I’ll Never Fill Out

Umete

How I test Umete’s form matching without filling out forms myself, what the test caught, and how much AI matching adds.

November 25, 2026 · Umete 0.5.2 · 220 forms from Chromium’s and Firefox’s autofill tests

Overview

Umete automatically fills complete web forms with answers saved from previous forms. Matching happens on your device first, from each field’s label, name, placeholder, and autocomplete hint. Fields it can’t place can go to an AI, which sees only descriptions of the fields, never your answers.

I don’t fill out many forms, so I don’t get to try Umete as often as my other apps. This note covers a test that replays real forms instead, how Umete does on it, and what AI matching adds on top of matching on your device.

The test

Browsers keep saved copies of real forms to test their own autofill. The test uses two sets:

  • Chromium’s 184 forms: checkout and sign-up pages from sites like Lowe’s, Macy’s, and Walmart, plus forms in 13 other languages, each with the type Chromium expects for every field.
  • Firefox’s 36 forms: checkout, shipping, and sign-in pages from Best Buy, Costco, Home Depot, Walmart, and others. Firefox’s tests list the fields Firefox finds without saying which element each is, so I lined them up with each page and checked every one by hand, correcting the ones Firefox gets wrong, like an address nickname it calls a street line.

The test runs Umete’s real extension scripts on all 220:

  1. A fictional person in San Francisco fills one sign-up form and chooses Remember my answers.
  2. Each saved form opens in WebKit, the engine behind Safari, with the page’s own scripts and all network access off, and Umete runs Fill this form.
  3. Every labeled field is checked: filled correctly, filled wrongly, or missed. A field the person has no answer for, like a middle name, should stay empty. Card numbers and passwords must never be filled.

A wrong value is worse than an empty field, since you might not notice it. So I look at two numbers: precision, how many of Umete’s fills were right, and recall, how many of the fields the person had an answer for got one.

No run filled a password or card number. Fields hidden on the saved page, and fields that already held the right value, aren’t counted. The person’s address was remembered from the same sign-up form as everything else. The one wrong fill on device is a phone field labeled “Phone (with area code)” that Chromium expects only the area code in.

What AI matching adds

After matching on your device, Umete asks the AI about the fields it left unhandled, one multiple-choice question per field: which saved answer belongs here, or none. It fills a field only when the AI is at least 60% sure. The test used OpenAI’s Decisions API with gpt-6-luna, the setup Umete uses by default.

  • Of the 564 fields matching on device missed, Umete asked the AI about 454, in 278 requests. Most of the rest it leaves empty on purpose, like a second phone number on the same page.
  • 314 of those got the right answer. Most of them, 200, are on forms in other languages, like German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, and Russian, whose labels matching on device often can’t place.
  • 13 got a wrong value. Four are arguably right: Spanish forms’ “first surname”, the first of two surnames, got the person’s one surname, and a Turkish “city” field Chromium expects a state in got the city. Six put the whole street line in a box for the street name alone. Three are plain mistakes, such as a street address in a First Name field.

AI matching adds less when the person’s address is saved in the app as a structured address instead: on all 220 forms, recall goes from 66.3% to 77.2%, at 99.7% precision. Umete fills structured addresses only on your device and never sends address fields to the AI, so their misses stay.

What the test caught

Each run found bugs I hadn’t run into myself.

Fixed in Umete 0.4.9

  • A field labeled just “Address” got your email address. This was the biggest source of wrong fills.
  • Address line 2 got your street, when the form you remembered had no second line to match it.
  • A phone number split into boxes for area code, prefix, and line number went whole into the first box. Umete now fills each box with its part.
  • When a dropdown sat inside its label, Umete remembered the label with every option in it, like “State Select a state Arizona California…”, which made the answer hard to match later.
  • A lone “Area code” box got the ZIP code, and ZIP+4 and phone extension boxes got answers too; they now stay empty.

Fixed in Umete 0.5.2

  • Older pages often put a field’s name in the table cell or box beside it instead of in a label, so Umete read no label at all. It now reads that text, which is where most of 0.5.2’s gain in recall comes from. Before, Crutchfield’s Company field, named “address2”, got your apartment.
  • A City field got the ZIP code, because the error message inside its label said “double-check the city, state, and ZIP code.” Umete now reads only the words before the field.
  • The ZIP code went into coupon, captcha, and “ZIP Extn” boxes because they share the word “code” or look like a ZIP field.
  • An “Area Code” box with no length limit got the whole phone number. It now gets the area code, and the box after it the rest.
  • The apartment went into both an Apt/Suite box and the second address line after it, and “Name” next to “Surname” got the full name instead of the first.

Each fix was checked against all the forms before it shipped, to see what it changed on other sites as well as the one it was for.

Limits

  • Chromium’s labels are its own predictions, kept to catch changes in its autofill, not answers someone checked by hand. A few are debatable, such as a gift recipient’s email labeled as the person’s. Firefox’s I checked by hand.
  • The pages are old saved copies that run no scripts, so the test doesn’t cover custom dropdowns, fields inside web components, or forms built as you go. I try to check those on live sites.
  • It’s one fictional person with a US address, and the AI results are from a single run. AI answers can differ a little from run to run.

The test is open source with the rest of Umete, in github.com/littlebobert/umete/eval.
